1. What We Found in the Existing Project Docs
The component plan already exists in multiple project documents. This specification consolidates those decisions, corrects one power-path ambiguity, and focuses the project specifically on A/C performance measurement.
1.1 Design correction: Nano ESP32 power path
The earlier architecture block showed a 12 V -> 5 V automotive buck feeding the Nano VIN/5 V path. The current Arduino Nano ESP32 documentation makes an important distinction: VIN is specified for 6-21 V, the board is 3.3 V I/O, and 5 V is available through VBUS only when supplied from USB-C. Therefore this specification supersedes the earlier 5 V-to-VIN interpretation.
Primary in-vehicle power: use a protected automotive regulator feeding approximately 6.5-7.0 V into Nano VIN, then validate brownout behavior during cranking.
Pressure sensors receive their own regulated 5 V rail; do not expect the VIN-powered Nano ESP32 to provide a 5 V sensor rail.
USB-C remains the preferred bench/service/debug data path and may also power the board on the bench.
2. Product Mission and Scope
Mount the electronics enclosure inside the engine bay on the body/chassis, not on refrigerant plumbing.
Measure A/C low-side and high-side pressure continuously while the vehicle is operating.
Add suction/liquid-line temperature and ambient/vent temperature so the system can move beyond pressure-only diagnosis.
Log raw observations locally to microSD and expose live telemetry over BLE to the in-vehicle Android infotainment device.
Provide a Wi-Fi SoftAP for service, logs, configuration, diagnostics and OTA updates without requiring internet.
Use USB-C for bench flashing/debugging and local recovery.
Calculate only evidence-backed A/C performance metrics; diagnostic hypotheses must expose the measurements and equations that produced them.
Integrate every service, sensor calibration, firmware version and A/C finding into the broader 4Runner Vehicle Health OS history.
2.1 Non-goals for the first vehicle build
Do not automatically charge, evacuate or vent refrigerant.
Do not claim refrigerant charge state from one pressure snapshot.
Do not leave inexpensive service-tool couplers as the final permanent product interface.
Do not depend on cloud connectivity for measurement or basic diagnosis.
Do not expose raw 12 V vehicle power or 5 V sensor output directly to 3.3 V ESP32 I/O.
3. Reference System Architecture
3.1 Data authority
The A/C node owns raw pressure/temperature observations and its own hardware-health data. The Android Vehicle Health OS owns cross-system fusion, long-term history, versioned equations, service records, and user-facing maintenance recommendations. Raw data remains authoritative and must be replayable after firmware or model changes.

5. Pressure Sensor Requirements and Interface
The current Sensata automotive A/C pressure-sensor platform is a strong match because it has dedicated low- and high-side variants, R134a compatibility, analog/LIN options, automotive environmental ratings, and separate operating ranges appropriate to each side.
5.1 Gauge vs. absolute pressure
The Sensata ranges are specified as absolute pressure (barA). The system should retain absolute pressure as the raw quantity. If it displays familiar manifold-style gauge pressure, convert using a documented atmospheric-pressure input:
Atmospheric pressure can come from a calibrated local pressure sensor, a configuration value for controlled testing, or another validated source. The source must be logged with each calculation run.
6. Automotive Power and Electrical Architecture
All Nano ESP32 GPIO/analog I/O is 3.3 V. Sensor outputs must be divided/clamped/conditioned before reaching the MCU or a 3.3 V-powered ADC.
USB-C can power the Nano at 5 V on the bench, but engine-bay primary power should use the protected vehicle-power path so USB remains available for service/debug.
The custom carrier must include brownout/reset telemetry so a crank-related power event is distinguishable from an A/C fault.
High and low pressure sensors should have independently switchable power so firmware can isolate/power-cycle a failed channel.
6.1 ADC recommendation
Use ADS1115-Q1 on the custom carrier unless bench data proves the Nano ADC is sufficiently repeatable. Four channels allow HIGH pressure, LOW pressure, a known calibration/reference channel and one spare analog input. The final analog front-end still needs input protection and scaling consistent with the ordered sensor output.
7. Refrigerant Plumbing and Engine-Bay Mechanical Design
7.1 Prototype / data-collection interface
1.  Connect the dedicated R134a LOW quick coupler to the vehicle low-side service port.
2.  Connect the dedicated R134a HIGH quick coupler to the vehicle high-side service port.
3.  Use short refrigerant-rated service hose or a mechanically supported adapter block so the sensor mass is not cantilevered from the aluminum A/C line.
4.  Adapt to the exact pressure-sensor thread using rated metal fittings and refrigerant-compatible seals.
5.  Leak-check the new joints before operating the vehicle and inspect again after heat/vibration cycling.
6.  Label HIGH and LOW physically and electrically so the channels cannot be swapped during service.
7.2 Permanent production interface
Replace service-tool couplers with an A/C-rated tee, dedicated sensor port, or OEM-style permanent pressure-sensor interface.
Preserve a normal service port so the system can still be recovered, evacuated, vacuum-tested and charged with standard equipment.
Any line modification must be professionally recovered/evacuated and leak-tested before return to service.
Do not use the sensor or adapter stack as a structural support for the electronics enclosure.
Keep the sensor wiring and refrigerant hardware clear of belts, fans, exhaust/manifold heat, sharp edges and engine-motion paths.

9.1 Minimum log schema

10. Power-On Self-Test and Continuous Built-In Test
1.  Read reset reason, hardware revision, firmware version and configuration CRC.
2.  Measure protected vehicle input, Nano VIN rail, sensor 5 V rail and 3.3 V rail.
3.  Measure a known ADC reference; if it is outside tolerance, mark pressure data INVALID rather than publishing trusted PSI.
4.  Power HIGH sensor, test for open/short/rail-stuck/implausible output, then sample variance.
5.  Repeat for LOW sensor.
6.  Verify temperature channels and detect obvious open/short/stuck behavior.
7.  Mount microSD, write random payload + CRC, close/reopen/read/verify/delete.
8.  Start BLE and verify local advertising/service health.
9.  Start Wi-Fi AP when requested and verify local HTTP /health.
10.  Persist POST result to NVM and SD. A newly updated firmware image becomes VALID only after critical POST passes.
11. Android Infotainment / Vehicle Health OS Integration
Primary daily link: BLE from the engine-bay node to the installed Android infotainment device.
Service fallback: Android or phone joins 4RUNNER-AC-XXXX SoftAP for logs, calibration, diagnostics and firmware update.
USB-C is a service/development path; direct USB-to-head-unit integration is optional and depends on the identified Android head unit USB host behavior.
Android stores device identity, hardware revision, sensor serial/configuration, calibration revision and every capture session.
The Vehicle Health OS fuses A/C node data with OBD/CAN context such as RPM, vehicle speed, compressor state and other validated HVAC signals.
Every top-level A/C health card must drill down to calculation run -> equation version -> exact inputs -> raw sensor samples.

12. Validation and A/C Test Protocol
12.1 Bench gate before vehicle install
Sweep input supply over the expected automotive operating window and validate deterministic brownout/recovery.
Inject known pressure-sensor voltages or a sensor simulator at multiple points; verify calibration and open/short detection.
Compare ADS1115-Q1 and Nano ADC results during bench calibration; decide whether production requires the external ADC.
Interrupt power repeatedly during SD writes; verify no unacceptable corruption and deterministic recovery.
Test BLE with enclosure closed and Wi-Fi AP/log download/OTA.
Run hot-soak chamber/enclosure test to the selected component/environment limits.
Run an intentionally bad OTA image and verify rollback/safe mode.
12.2 Vehicle baseline sequence
1.  Record ambient temperature, atmospheric pressure, vehicle state and firmware/calibration versions before A/C start.
2.  With engine off, capture equalized/static high and low pressures when practical.
3.  Start engine and A/C under a repeatable configuration; record RPM, blower/recirc state and initial pressure/temperature transient.
4.  Wait for stabilization; record high/low pressure, ambient, suction/liquid line temperatures and vent temperature.
5.  Repeat at elevated idle if safe/appropriate and then under controlled road operation.
6.  Record low-speed/idle and moving airflow conditions to characterize condenser-airflow sensitivity.
7.  Repeat over multiple ambient temperatures/days to establish the vehicle-specific healthy baseline.
8.  Compare the node against trusted manifold/service equipment before promoting the sensor calibration to VEHICLE_VALIDATED.
13. Development Plan and Immediate Next Steps
13.1 Definition of Done - first installed prototype
Boots reliably from switched vehicle power and survives engine crank without corrupting logs.
Passes POST and identifies its board/firmware/calibration revision.
Reads high and low pressure from validated refrigerant-compatible sensors.
Logs timestamped telemetry to microSD with device-health fields.
Streams live data to the Android device over BLE with repeatable reconnect behavior.
Exposes local Wi-Fi /health, logs and OTA/recovery workflow.
USB-C bench/service access remains functional.
No measurable refrigerant leak from the prototype interface.
A/C performance calculations are traceable to raw observations and equation versions.
The complete build can be reproduced from a version-controlled BOM, wiring/pin map, firmware and calibration package.

Diagnostic truth boundary
Pressure alone is not sufficient to declare low charge, overcharge, restriction, weak compressor or condenser-airflow failure. The software may generate ranked hypotheses, but charge-state or component-fault confidence must increase only when pressure, temperature, compressor state, ambient/airflow context, and trend evidence agree.
